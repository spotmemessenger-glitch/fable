The photo leads — Telegram's call, and right here

  • A full-bleed hero with the name overlaid, not WhatsApp's small centred circle. In a proximity app the photo is the identity — it is what someone sees on the map before deciding to say hello, so it earns the space.
  • Multiple photos, with Telegram's page dots at the top. One picture is a thin basis for a stranger to judge a meeting.
  • Camera and edit sit as floating buttons on the photo, so the rows below stay purely navigational.
  • Keet's contribution is restraint: no badges, no gamification, three honest counts.

Why Settings is ordered this way

  • Safety first, always. Ghost mode, map visibility, location precision, who can find you, blocked users. These get reached in a hurry — three taps deep is a safety failure, not a taste one.
  • Language is its own section. It is the product's differentiator, and packs need managing — ML Kit is roughly 30 MB per pair, so "4 · 118 MB" must be visible and deletable.
  • Cloned voice lives in Profile, not Settings — it is an asset you own, like your photo, not a preference you flip. One voice, as specified.
  • Bluetooth gets a row because it is a transport with real battery and permission cost.

Two pieces of copy that must stay honest

  • The cloned-voice note is deliberately on the card. It is the one feature that sends your data off-device, contradicting the on-device-only rule the rest of the app follows. Hiding that in a privacy policy would be the dishonest option.
  • "Disappearing messages" needs a subtitle inside its page: the timer is cooperative, not enforced. A modified client keeps whatever it already received. Telegram implies more than it delivers — say what is true.
