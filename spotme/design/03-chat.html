Why the split bubble is right

  • No toggle. Source and translation are both present, always. Nothing to find, no mode to be in, no way to be looking at the wrong one.
  • The original is never discarded. Translation is a derived view rendered beneath it — which matches the P2P model, where each peer translates on arrival and the message keeps its source-language tag.
  • It survives a bad translation. If the machine gets it wrong, the reader can see that it did. A toggle hides the evidence.
  • Italic on the translation plus a hairline divider carry the distinction without a second colour.

Details from the reference

  • Header carries distance and presence — "12 m away · Online". This is a proximity app, so how far away someone is belongs beside their name.
  • Three header actions: transliterate (globe), translate (active), voice.
  • Outgoing timestamp sits outside the bubble on the left, with the read receipt below-right.
  • The AI notice is a system line, not a bubble — it is not part of the conversation.

One thing worth deciding

  • Your outgoing messages show only English. Roberto reads them in Spanish, but you never see what he actually receives — so you cannot catch a translation that changes your meaning.
  • The fix is the same split applied outbound: your text on top, "TRANSLATED TO SPANISH" beneath. It costs vertical space on every message you send, which is presumably why the reference omits it. Worth a deliberate call rather than an accident.
